# Text

> Therefore, if your employee has this finger long, you can safely entrust him with control over, and charge of others. You will be amazed how well he or she will make rules and regulations and see that they are obeyed; but beware, Mr. Employer, lest your first finger is short in proportionas that of your employee is long, for, if such be the case, you too willhave "to toe the line" and you may find yourself in a very disagreeable position. (*Palmistry For All*)

My talk with Pippin yesterday reaffirmed my feeling that I'm moving generally in the right direction. We discussed ways in that the text could be different each time, but that the sliders might noticably select different templates that are themselves randomized. The idea is that the player feels like the sliders have some relationship with the fate text, but not in a way that gives them control over fate through their control over the sliders.

My thought is that the sliders could be turned into pretty simple bools depending on the side of 50% that they sit on.

Pippin suggested that I write out some sample fates first before I work them into templates, which is how I usually work anyway. I'm going to try to pick through some of the palmistry and maybe phrenology texts I found both for flavor and in the case of the palmistry texts, supposed correlations between hand lines and fate.

I'm imagining something maybe future tense, like:

> You have a character possessing of coarse instincts, devoid of real intellectuality, and belonging to the lower order of humanity. As a born murderer, you will live a short and brutal life punctuated at times by brief moments of happiness borne from the riches you ripped from the hands of those more deserving than you. During a daring and deranged assault on a local brewery, death comes swiftly when you are subdued by law enforcers armed with truncheon and poleaxe.

(but longer, more spurious details, body part injury, etc)

I'm adding chunks of text from the palmistry books to the corpora folder, probably not to use directly as markov sources or anything but to pick through for the supposed function of each line in order to work out which templates each line's axes should select.

Obviously I don't want to bother with color or branchiness or interrupted lines or any of the other details in these texts, I just want to produce a basic idea of the domain of each line and maybe an axis of two qualities or outcomes from each one. I don't want to have to make dozens of templates.

Reading this stuff I am immediately envisioning the phrenology head prototype and what that will look like. It's going to be a quick and brutal joke, you punch a head amd deform it and are instantly given a set of dungeon and dragon stats with the punch sound, no descriptive text. Being quantified is a kind of violence.

This passage from *Palmistry for All* reminds me of how big companies still make employees do Meyers-Briggs personality tests in order to classify and manage them a completely spurious test with no basis in any rigorous discipline, HR department astrology. Evidently they used to do this with *palm readings*—

> There is no country in the world where the "study of character" is more indulged in than in the United States of America. During my many visits there I could not help remarking how even the "hardest headed" business men used any form of this study that they could get hold of to help them in their business dealings with other men and also in endeavouring to ascertain the character of their clerks and employees.

> In looking over the records of my career I find that in the course of my visits to America I gave private lessons to the heads of two hundred and seventy business establishments in New York, one hundred and thirty-five in Boston, and three hundred and forty-two in Chicago.

> All these men were large employers of labour and what they principally wanted was, to have some help beyond that of their own judgment in dealing with those with whom they came in contact in the regular course of their business careers. In no other country did I find the same interest taken in the study of character from a practical standpoint.

> Everyone knows that "the face can wear a mask," that a person may be a good actor and put on a certain expression that may deceive even the best judgment.

> But hands cannot change as the result of a mere effort to please; *the character they express is the real nature of the individual* --the true character that has been formed by heredity or that has grown up with the person by long years of habit.

One thing I noticed in *Palmistry for All*— differences between left and right hand lines are noted as important, and we only see the left hand. A hidden right hand could be a confounding random factor I suppose, but I'd have to find a way to communicate that, which is as overcomplicated as having a second hand on-screen.

I am wondering how hard it would be to vary the shapes of the lines with the random button along with the hand background png's. I feel like it would be a lot of work and since the player has control of that random button it's not a confounding element. As long as they don't press random again or touch any slider they'd expect to get the same result. The biggest bang for my buck is that every sumbit gets a random result but that the sliders have some obvious connection to which template gets used.

After going through the palmistry texts and trying to extract useful information, I can see that they all depend way too much on qualities I am not modeling, line direction variation, branchiness, separations in lines, things that would be a lot of work to include. I can pretty much arbitrarily connect templates to axes of lines and maybe only pay attention to the primary lines for head and heart in terms of template content. I should just go ahead and make some arbitrary templates and not spend a week trying to tease out connections. This is going to be mostly inscrutable... there's a happy medium between being totally random and actually modelling a method of "accurate" palm reading, which is a practice that is purposely ultra-complex and opaque in order to cover its own ass, like any "magic technology".

What these texts seem to be really useful for is victorian sounding summaries of character, which can be used as template starters. I think it might be funny to mix these with some corporate myers-briggs personality type summaries. I also found and scraped an incredible list of victorian job titles that I am going to replace my regular careers list with.

Right now I have a list of generated fantasy names, but maybe these should be replaced with victorian names? go all in?

The question of tense is a big one that will make the majority of text cleaning work. Right now I am imagining that only "born" summaries "you were born a poor child" etc are past tense and that everything else is future tense? So like the moment you clicked play you were born and your coming fate was sealed, forclosing the whole game.

Adding some text from a US Air Force Myers-Briggs testing document and one from Architects designing offices. > ...Klages and Jung; both made common cause with Fascism. (*On Some Motifs in Baudelaire*)

and of mythology:

> The fairy tale tells us of the earliest arrangements that mankind made to shake off the nightmare which the myth had placed upon its chest. (*The Storyteller*)

The Jung connection is always made as being a coincidence, but if it's just a coincidence, why not point to Fourier's *Phalansterie* instead? Because he is identified as a crackpot where as Jung gets continually refreshed by a retrograde religious longing for some kind of prehistoric fantasy reality, the kind so often surfaced in the world of videogames and genre media (*cough*starwars*cough*), with a patina of serious science, as if serious science isn't also an infinite march of crackpots.
