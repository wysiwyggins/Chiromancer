# Chance

I added some text lists to play with in tracery but I haven't made any decisions about whether the text generation is purely random, deterministic, or a combination (I know nothing on a computer is purely random, you know what I mean.) I'm meeting with Pippin tomorrow so I'm saving that for conversation.

What if the text generation went all together? does it make more sense if it just says "GAME OVER"? Am I doing random text because it's in my well-worn bag of things-I-do?

No, I think the text does the work of saying "this is how you will die." Instead of just feeling like a bug/broken game.

What I did add was some randomness still within the player control- a random button that varies the sliders and also changes the shape of the hand subtly.

One idea I am considering is randomness outside of the player's control, like an assigned name. This would let me make the game somewhat deterministic (but why does that matter? Only I and my cohort/instructor would know...) but still out of the player's control. I can't shake the feeling that the sliders should connect with the text output in a way that doesn't matter/isn't useful. But it would effectively still be the same as totally random in its player effect...

Lopping off a finger seemed like it was in the realm of fate, not character, so I didn't include that.

A thought while reading the Tufte powerpoint essay, when stumbling over a slide reading in big letters 'correlation is not causation' and Tufte bemoaning that this is a longer maxim shortened by low resolution pp slides...
*Character and Fate*—  in the realm of metaphysics character and fate can not have a causal relationship, in our world, across regimes, the bridge that was attempted between the two ideas was statistics. Eugenics is a demonic shadow of mythological thinking that haunts statistics. These sorts of demonic residues thrive in videogames, as a game world is computable in a way that our own stubborn world refuses to be. That's the connection I am hoping to install within my game. A forced connection between stats and moribund mythological ideas of fate, which is the "guilt-context of the living" (Benjamin et al. 1978)
